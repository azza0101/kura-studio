GLOBAL KURA STYLE & RULES
Rough handmade 2D layered paper-cut animation. Characters, animals, props and scenery are built from bold irregular geometric cut-paper shapes and visibly stacked flat layers. Realistic tactile paper textures: coarse fibres, torn edges, uneven scissor cuts, creases, scuffed ink and imperfect printed colour. Small contact shadows separate the paper layers; the composition stays graphic and flat, never glossy 3D or live-action. Hand-positioned graphic stop-motion movement with slight registration shifts and crisp, deliberate poses. At each scene opening, physical paper pieces slide, drop or hinge into place in a short visible assembly, forming one coherent full-frame scene.
Everything is a flat cut-paper shape seen straight-on, orthographic front view, zero perspective depth.
Every surface is flat uniform-colour printed paper with visible paper grain, coarse fibres and slight crease lines.
Edges are crisp scissor-cut and knife-cut contours; slightly irregular, slightly wobbly, never smooth vector curves.
Layers: background, midground and foreground are separate stacked sheets of paper, each sharply in focus.
Shadows: ONLY a thin hard-edged flat contact shadow directly under each paper layer, one solid darker tone, never soft.
Colours: flat, slightly desaturated, imperfectly printed, with small misregistration.
Light fixtures, windows, screens and glows are drawn as flat coloured paper cutouts, not light sources.
No blur, no depth of field, no bokeh, no background defocus.
No soft shadows, no gradients, no airbrush shading, no vignette, no glow, no light rays, no volumetric light, no lens effects.
No 3D volume, no realistic rendering, no digital painting, no smooth vector look.
Narrative viewpoints may change through redrawn flat paper compositions; the paper artwork itself remains front-facing and orthographic, with no volumetric perspective.
Use sequential instant hard cuts between full-frame shots. Selective very slight push-ins, tiny lateral adjustments, small follow movements and brief graphic punch-ins may guide attention or strengthen a reveal. Keep camera movement minimal, simple and slightly stepped, like hand repositioning between stop-motion frames. Static holds are allowed when useful. No smooth cinematic glides, orbit or arc moves, large dollies, sweeping pans, complex parallax or fluid interpolation.
Vertical 9:16. Keep essential action and reactions slightly above the frame centre. Keep the lower-middle area visually quiet for subtitles added later, without a visible subtitle band. Show one coherent full-frame scene at a time. No split screen, inset panels, picture-in-picture or multi-panel layouts; physical layered paper collage is allowed.
Preserve each character's identity, face, hair, clothing and geographic specificity throughout the shots, except for explicitly scripted changes. Use supplied references only for their assigned roles; reference characters do not bring their original page layout into the scene. Show exactly one instance of each character listed as present in a shot. Add no unrequested extras. Definitions do not imply presence in every shot.
Match the illustration style of the supplied style reference throughout every scene: preserve its flat cut-paper construction, graphic contours, tactile printed-paper texture, earthy colour palette and simple character design. Use the reference as art direction; do not reproduce its layout, lettering, logos or unrelated characters and objects. Depict only the characters and elements specified in the scene, with their own distinct identities and features; preserve them consistently across shots.
Character differentiation: Whenever multiple characters appear, give every text-defined character a clearly distinct silhouette, hairstyle, hair colour, clothing palette and facial proportions compared with all reference characters. Avoid visual resemblance or identity blending, especially between the protagonist and secondary characters. Maintain these differences consistently across all shots and all three variants.
Keep unique objects unique, preserve their colours and owners, and show scripted handovers in the correct shot. Preserve entrances, exits, appearances, disappearances, gestures, gazes, reactions, silent visual gags and animal actions in their narrative order. Animals and secondary characters appear only where the scene specifies them. Do not remove them to satisfy a generic no-extras rule or duplicating them during an entrance. Physical paper assemblies occur where appropriate within the allotted shot time, without rebuilding the set at every cut in the same location.
Whenever a scene includes an emotional change, end on a close-up of the affected character's face. Make the change clearly visible through specific changes in eyes, eyebrows and mouth, rather than body language alone. Keep the face readable and allow the final expression to register.
Use only environmental sounds and SFX. No speech, no voices, no spoken words, no dialogue, no voiceover, no lip sync. No music unless explicitly requested in non_diegetic_music. No generated text, logos, numbers or captions unless the scene explicitly specifies an exception. No 3D, live-action or realistic scene rendering; tactile realism applies only to the paper material.

REGOLA PERMANENTE — EYES

All characters MUST have strictly flat, solid-colour cartoon eyes with absolutely NO visible white sclera. NEVER draw white eyeballs, white areas around pupils, white eye highlights, white reflections, or realistic eye anatomy. Eyes must match the original KURA reference style: simple dark graphic shapes integrated into the face colour. This rule applies to EVERY character, in EVERY shot, including extreme close-ups and exaggerated facial expressions.
